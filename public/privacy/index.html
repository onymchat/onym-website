Privacy Policy

We don’t collect
your data.

Onym is built so there is nothing to collect. No account, no phone number, no email, no tracking, no ads, no analytics. Your identity and your conversations live on your device — not on ours.

Last updated 19 July 2026

The short version

  • >We don’t ask who you are. There is no sign-up, no phone number, and no email.
  • >We run no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party tracking SDKs.
  • >Your keys, contacts, and messages are generated and stored on your device.
  • >Messages are end-to-end encrypted. We never see their contents.

What we collect

Nothing. Onym has no servers that store your data and no account system that could hold it. Creating an identity happens entirely on your phone; it produces cryptographic keys, not a profile in a database we own.

We do not collect names, phone numbers, email addresses, contact lists, location, device identifiers, usage statistics, or crash reports. There is no Onym analytics pipeline, no advertising, and no third-party tracking or attribution SDK embedded in the app.

What stays on your device

Your private keys, your recovery phrase, your contacts, your group memberships, and your message history are stored locally on your device. Your twelve-word recovery phrase leaves the device only if you choose to write it down or copy it.

We cannot read any of it. We have no copy of it. If you delete the app or lose your device without your recovery phrase, that data is gone — we cannot restore it for you, because we never had it.

What third parties can see

Onym is decentralized, so your messages travel over infrastructure we don’t own. We’d rather be honest about that than pretend it doesn’t exist:

Relays and media servers. Messages and invitations move through Nostr relays; media (images, video, voice) is stored on Blossom servers. They carry ciphertext — never plaintext — but a relay operator can see connection metadata such as IP address and timing. You can point the app at your own relays and servers, or self-host them, for maximum privacy.

The Stellar network + the relayer. Group membership is anchored on the public Stellar network via a smart contract; a relayer submits the signed transactions and pays the fees. These see ciphertext and on-chain commitments — not your messages, and not which member sent a given message. Run your own relayer and switch to it any time.

Your app store and OS. Downloading and updating the app goes through Apple’s App Store (or Google Play on Android), which have their own privacy policies outside our control.

For the full accounting of who sees what, read the threat model.

Data requests

We have no accounts, no message store, and no social graph. If we receive a legal request for user data, our honest answer is that we have nothing to produce. The only shared state is the public smart contract, which anyone can already read.

Children

Onym is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone — because it collects no information from anyone at all.
